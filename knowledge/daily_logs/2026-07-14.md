# Daily Log — 2026-07-14

## Trend Scout: 75 Deep backlog inspiration

### Trend 1 — Slowed/vintage-groove edit wave
"Golden Brown" (slowed) by The Stranglers is trending hard on Reels (40+ yr old track, new life via slowed pitch + dreamy visuals). TikTok's broader "slowed funk/disco edit" tag is running alongside it — creators are pitching down boogie/disco cuts to expose warm basslines and vintage grooves for night-drive and mood content.
- **How to flip it:** Take one of the nearly-finished 75 Deep tracks and cut a slowed "dub/alt" edit (-4 to -6% pitch, extra reverb tail, stripped drums) as a teaser clip. Matches the "night-drive, sunset, warm bassline" visual language already trending — good fit for a track-reveal Reel without touching the main mix.

### Remix target — D Train, "You're the One for Me" (West End Records, 1981)
- **Why on-brand:** Classic NYC boogie/funk — tight looping bassline, live drums, sweaty falsetto vocal. Same DNA as the Strictly Rhythm/Nervous/West End lineage Unkle Funk already draws from. Deep-cut enough to feel like a crate-digger find, not a worn-out sample (unlike Chic/Rufus-level classics).
- **How to flip it:** Loop the 8-bar bass hook, drop it under a swung 4/4 kick + deep house chords (Larry Heard style), pitch the vocal down slightly for a "slowed groove" edge that rides the current slowed-disco trend. Quick version: 90-second loop edit for a Reel; full version: a proper 75 Deep track later.
- **Trending potential:** Rides the slowed/vintage-groove edit wave and the broader boogie/nu-disco revival, while staying true to the anti-formulaic, crate-digger identity (not EDM, not commercial).
