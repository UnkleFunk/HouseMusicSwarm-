# Daily Log — 2026-07-18

[TREND] Trend scout for 75 Deep backlog (auto-run, 2026-07-18)

## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Aretha Franklin — "Respect" (1967) is back on Reels/TikTok**
via a lip-sync format where creators mouth "what you want, baby, I got it"
over product/service reveals. It's a straight soul classic riding the
broader disco/funk/soul revival that's currently pulling Earth, Wind & Fire's
"September" and the Bee Gees' "Stayin' Alive" back into rotation — the whole
lineage 75 Deep already draws from is having a cultural moment right now,
not just this one track.
- **How to use it:** don't touch the Aretha vocal itself (too recognizable,
  too legally hot) — instead borrow the *format*: a confident vocal hook
  answering a "what do you want" prompt, chopped and gated into a
  call-and-response stab under a 122–124 BPM groove. Works as a teaser edit
  for whichever 75 Deep track has a horn or vocal stab with that same
  "here's your answer" swagger.

**2. The "summoning hand-sign → hard cut on the beat drop" reveal format**
(TikTok's #1 trend for June, still running hot into July, 36M+ posts,
borrowed from Jujutsu Kaisen, now used for everything from food reveals to
outfit reveals). It's a pure edit template, not a sample — but the mechanic
(tension gesture → hard cut exactly on a drop) maps directly onto a 75 Deep
release strategy: build 8 bars of filtered tension, gesture/blackout, hard
cut into the full drum groove. A 12–15 sec clip of just that cut is
native to the format and doesn't need new music, just a smart edit point
on a track that's already close to done.

## Remix target: Cheryl Lynn — "Got to Be Real" (Columbia, 1978)

- **On-brand why:** disco-funk with a bassline that's practically Chicago
  house scripture already — it's been chopped, flipped, and interpolated
  across deep/soulful house for decades (the "sounds like Unkle Funk would
  have played it" test from the taste profile passes easily). Sexy,
  confident vocal hook ("got to be real") gives it the same call-and-response
  quality as the Aretha trend above, so it doubles as a hook for that format
  too.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the "got to be real" vocal + the
  Fender Rhodes stabs, rebuild the low end as a warm 120–123 BPM deep house
  groove (C minor or A minor pocket to sit near the "1998" fingerprint),
  keep the original bass guitar riff panned wide under the new sub rather
  than replacing it outright — it's the most recognizable part of the record
  and the whole reason the flip reads as a flip, not a bootleg.
- **Trending potential:** rides the disco/funk/soul revival that's already
  pulling classic-era records back into TikTok/Reels rotation this month,
  and the "got to be real" hook is short, iconic, and lip-sync-ready —
  exactly the shape of clip that travels on these platforms.
