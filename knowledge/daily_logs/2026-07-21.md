## Remix target: Marvin Gaye — "Sexual Healing" (1982)

- **On-brand why:** Funky *and* genuinely sexy — the exact register the
  task brief asked for — and it's not just a vocal, it's a production
  landmark: Gaye programmed the beat himself on a Roland CR-78, one of
  the first drum machines on a major soul record. That's a direct line
  to the "dusty drum machines, 909/808/LinnDrum energy" DNA in the taste
  profile, not just a horn-stab flip. Sits comfortably in Larry
  Levan/Paradise Garage-adjacent territory — the kind of record that
  would've gotten played *and* sampled in that room.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the CR-78 rimshot-and-clave pattern plus
  the "baby, I'm hot just like an oven" vocal ad-lib as a 2-bar loop.
  Filter-house intro (low-pass sweep reveal on the original beat), let it
  ride solo for 4 bars, then bring in a warm sub bassline in the "1998"
  C-minor fingerprint under it at 122–124 BPM — keep Gaye's original
  drum pattern mostly intact rather than replacing it, since the CR-78
  groove *is* the hook. Hook-only edit, one reveal moment, not a full
  re-record — same restraint as the Candi Staton and EWF flips logged the
  past two weeks, keeps it a release-safe personal/preview edit.
- **Trending potential:** rides directly on this week's retro-soul wave
  (Tyler/Aretha) without needing to build recognition from zero, and the
  "sexy classic soul, flipped warm and low-end-heavy" angle is a clean
  contrast to the trap/hype-cue sports edits currently crowding the
  algorithm — different lane, same moment.
