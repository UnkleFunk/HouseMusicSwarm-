## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Aretha Franklin's "Respect" is resurging on Reels as an "own your
power" anthem trend** — creators are pairing the horn-and-vocal hook with
self-respect / boundary-setting storytime clips rather than a dance
format. It's a soul-power *emotional register*, not a specific dance
move, which is the useful part: it's a template ("gospel-funk hook =
confidence statement") that any 75 Deep track with a strong horn or
call-and-response vocal stab can borrow the framing from, without needing
to touch the Aretha master itself.
- **How to use it:** if a 75 Deep track has a horn stab or a
  gospel-adjacent vocal ad-lib, cut the moment right where the hook lands
  as a 10–15 sec "this is my Monday energy" clip — caption it as a
  confidence/boundary statement instead of a groove reveal. Rides the
  same emotional wave as the Respect resurgence without competing for the
  same sound.

**2. Michael Jackson vocal-cover trend ("I Treated You Bad") is pulling
funk/soul singing-cover content back into rotation** — creators doing
straight vocal covers over the original groove, which signals renewed
appetite for classic funk/soul vocal phrasing as content, not just
sample-flip content. Useful signal for which decade's *vocal energy*
currently reads as fresh rather than dated.
- **How to use it:** if any 75 Deep vocal take leans into MJ-adjacent
  phrasing (breathy ad-libs, rhythmic vocal stutters), a "cover vs.
  original groove" side-by-side snippet is a low-effort process clip that
  taps the same appetite.

## Remix target: Chaka Khan (with Rufus) — "Ain't Nobody" (1983)

- **On-brand why:** synth-bass hook + Chaka's vocal power sits right in
  the "owning your power" emotional lane the Aretha "Respect" trend is
  currently mining, but it's a different track — squarely 80s, funky and
  genuinely sexy (the original slow-build seduction arrangement), and
  it's already deep-catalog-trusted crate material (constantly sampled
  across deep/soulful house lineage Unkle Funk pulls from).
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the "ain't nobody love me better" vocal
  hook + the signature synth-bass riff as a 2–4 bar phrase, strip the
  original drum machine, rebuild under it with a 909/808 groove in the
  118–124 BPM pocket, keep the Rhodes-adjacent chord stabs low in the mix
  so the vocal stays the lead. One hook-only drop, not a full re-record —
  same release-safe restraint as the Candi Staton and EWF edits already
  logged, personal/preview use only.
- **Trending potential:** rides directly on top of the live
  Aretha-"Respect"-as-anthem wave (same "confidence/power" emotional
  register, adjacent enough to draw the same discovery pool) while still
  being its own distinct flip — low risk of getting buried under the
  Respect original.
