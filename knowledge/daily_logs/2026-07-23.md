## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Aretha Franklin — "Respect" is resurging on Instagram Reels**, currently
used as a business/product lip-sync trend built around the line "What you
want, baby, I got it." This isn't a random pop resurgence — it's a soul
classic re-entering the discovery algorithm, which is direct leverage for
the gospel-soul lineage already baked into the 75 Deep sound (the Kerri
Chandler / Dennis Ferrer "vocal supporting the groove" DNA).
- **How to use it:** whichever 75 Deep track has a female vocal chop or a
  gospel-tinged stab in the breakdown, cut a 15–20 sec clip that leads with
  the vocal a cappella (or near-a cappella) for 2 bars before the beat drops
  — mirroring the "What you want, baby, I got it" reveal cadence Reels is
  already rewarding. Caption it as a lineage nod ("this is the same church
  Aretha's in") rather than trying to use "Respect" itself.

**2. The Dai Dai Dance Challenge (Shakira x Burna Boy, riding World Cup
momentum) is pulling percussive, Afrobeat-adjacent rhythm content into the
mainstream feed** alongside the still-live vintage funk/soul dance-edit
wave from last week. Simple, repeatable call-and-response choreography is
what's converting — not vocal complexity.
- **How to use it:** this is a rhythm-first opportunity, not a sample-flip
  one. If any 75 Deep track leans on Afro House–style percussive layering
  (per the taste profile's "rhythmic complexity over deep house
  foundations"), a 10–15 sec loop of just the percussion + bass breakdown,
  posted as a "count this groove" clip, rides the same call-and-response
  engagement pattern without competing head-on with the World Cup tie-in
  audio.

## Remix target: Aretha Franklin — "Rock Steady" (1971)

- **On-brand why:** Chuck Rainey's bassline is one of the funkiest, most
  sampled basslines in soul history — syncopated, rubbery, sits exactly in
  the "physically moves the floor" pocket the taste profile calls out for
  Kerri Chandler. Horn stabs, "sock it to me now" ad-libs, and gospel-rooted
  vocal power place it squarely in the same lineage as Frankie Knuckles/
  Trax-era Chicago house — it passes the "sounds like Unkle Funk would've
  played it" test easily, and it's a different Aretha cut than the one
  currently trending, so it reads as a deeper reference rather than a
  copycat.
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the "Rock Steady, Rock Steady" call-and-
  response hook (2 bars) as the vocal drop, filter-house intro (low-pass
  sweep bringing the horn stabs in first), then swap Rainey's bassline for
  a warm sub in the 75 Deep "1998" fingerprint key (C minor) at 122–124
  BPM — keep the horn hits as punctuation, not a loop. Hook-only edit, one
  drop, same release-safe restraint as the Candi Staton and EWF flips
  logged in prior weeks.
- **Trending potential:** direct halo effect from Aretha's live "Respect"
  resurgence on Reels — anyone algorithmically primed for Aretha content
  right now is a plausible discovery path for a "Rock Steady" flip, without
  needing to build recognition from zero.
