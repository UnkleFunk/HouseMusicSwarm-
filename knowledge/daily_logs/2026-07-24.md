[TREND] Trend scout for 75 Deep backlog (auto-run, 2026-07-24)

## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. "Nostalgia remix" format — short, upbeat remixes of older songs paired
with glow-up / then-vs-now content is one of the biggest audio-editing
patterns on TikTok and Reels right now.** The reporting frames it around
early-2000s/2010s pop, but the *mechanic* — take a recognizable old hook,
give it a punchier upbeat remix treatment, cut it against nostalgic or
transformation visuals — is genre-agnostic. Almost nobody in that lane is
reaching back to 70s/80s boogie-funk for the source material, which is
actually an opening: a 75 Deep flip built on a genuinely vintage sample
reads as more "authentic crate-dig" than the 2000s-pop remixes flooding
the same format, without needing to fight for the same recognition.
- **How to use it:** pick a nearly-finished 75 Deep track with a strong
  Rhodes/chord-stab hook, cut a 15-sec "old song, new groove" reveal —
  a few seconds of the original vintage sample straight, then a hard cut
  into the house groove underneath it. Caption it as the flip, not the
  final mix — same "here's the process" framing that's currently
  outperforming polished final-mix clips.

**2. The "summoning hand-sign → hard cut on the beat drop" reveal format
(anime-inspired, 36M+ posts, TikTok's current #1 trend) has normalized
one specific edit grammar: a held gesture, then a hard cut exactly on a
beat hit to reveal something.** Sonically it's off-brand — it's built
around EDM-style trap drops, not this catalog's patient, no-drop
arrangement philosophy — but the *edit grammar* (anticipation held, then
a clean cut landed precisely on a downbeat) is a free technique. Applied
to a 75 Deep clip, the "reveal" doesn't have to be a peak-energy drop —
it can be the moment a vintage vocal sample resolves into the track's
chord stab, landing on beat 1 the same way the trend's food/drink reveal
lands on the cut.
- **How to use it:** use the held-anticipation → hard-cut structure for a
  short clip that reveals a sample's *origin* — a second of dusty vinyl
  crackle, then cut clean into the finished 75 Deep groove. Keep the
  actual music untouched by drop culture; only borrow the cut timing.

## Remix target: Sylvia Striplin — "You Can't Turn Me Away" (1981)

- **On-brand why:** Produced by Roy Ayers on Concept East, this is
  deep-house bedrock — moody, minor-key, spiritual-not-commercial boogie
  that's already foundational DNA for the genre (its bassline underpins
  Blaze's "My Beat," a Chicago/NY deep house cornerstone). It's warm,
  dusty, and sexy rather than disco-shiny, which lines up with the taste
  profile's "warm · dusty · hypnotic · spiritual" word list far more
  precisely than a straight disco pick would.
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the verse's Rhodes-adjacent electric piano
  stab and the breathy vocal ad-libs as a 2–4 bar hook, run a slow
  low-pass filter reveal over the intro (no EDM-style riser), then swap
  the original bassline for a warm sub in the 75 Deep "1998" fingerprint
  key (C minor) at 122–124 BPM. Treat it as a hook-only edit — one loop,
  one filter-open moment, no build/drop — the same restraint used on the
  Candi Staton and EWF flips logged in previous weeks.
- **Trending potential:** rides the nostalgia-remix format directly (old
  hook, new groove, glow-up-adjacent visual) while standing out from the
  2000s/2010s pop remixes crowding that lane right now — a genuinely
  vintage soul source is the differentiator, not a liability.
