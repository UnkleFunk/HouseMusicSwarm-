## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. "Melancholic vintage vocal → hard beat-switch drop" is the dominant
transition format right now, via @neverbadagain's trap remix of the
Jackson 5's "I Treated You Bad"** — modern 808s under untouched MJ-style
vocal phrasing, three unrelated creators independently building the same

mechanic that's working isn't the specific song — it's the *contrast*:
hold a moody, almost-bare vintage vocal for tension, then cut hard into a
low end that doesn't belong to the original record.
- **How to use it:** pick whichever nearly-finished 75 Deep track has the

  buildup riser, just a straight cut. Frame the clip as the "beat switch"
  reveal moment rather than a full track preview; that's the exact shape
  this trend is rewarding right now.

  attention wave without touching the World Cup content itself.

## Remix target: D-Train — "You're the One for Me" (1982)

- **On-brand why:** this is *the* Larry Levan/Paradise Garage record that
  fed directly into early Chicago house DJ sets — horn-driven boogie
  groove, a killer repeated vocal hook, and a bassline built as melody
  (the exact "bass as melody" principle already logged in the DNA notes).
  It's sexy, funky, and it's Chicago house lineage in the most literal
  sense rather than an adjacent genre being borrowed from.
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the "you're the one, you're the one for
  me" hook over just the original bassline for 4–8 bars (the moody, bare
  section this week's beat-switch trend wants), then hard-cut into a
  rebuilt 122–124 BPM house groove in the 75 Deep "1998" fingerprint key,
  new sub bass under the original horn stabs left intact on top. Hook-only
  edit, one drop, same restraint as the Candi Staton and EWF flips already
  logged — release-safe as a personal/preview edit.
- **Trending potential:** double leverage — it's a ready-made fit for the
  beat-switch/contrast format blowing up this week, and it's authentic
  Chicago-house-origin material rather than a borrowed sound, so it reads
  as "on brand" rather than trend-chasing.
