## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Classic soul catalog resurgence — Aretha Franklin's "Respect" and
"(You Make Me Feel Like) A Natural Woman" are back on the FYP**, driven
by empowerment-edit and workout-playlist algorithmic pushes plus biopic/
documentary press. This is the same "vintage soul/funk genre wave" logged
on 7/20 (EWF, Brick House) still running, but the center of gravity has
shifted specifically toward Aretha's horn-blast, gospel-belt catalog —
i.e. exactly the "gospel undertones, lived-in vocal" lane in the music
context file, not generic nostalgia.
- **How to use it:** this is a technique trend, not a single-song trend —
  creators are pairing horn-blast soul hooks with unapologetic/confidence
  framing. Any nearly-finished 75 Deep track with a horn stab or a
  call-and-response vocal fragment can be cut into a 15-sec "horn hit
  drops on the beat" reveal clip and posted into the empowerment-edit /
  soul-revival tag cluster rather than needing a dedicated new audio.

**2. The "discovering something so good you instantly become its biggest
fan" reaction template (built off a viral Drake interview clip) is
outperforming polished final-mix posts right now** — people are using it
to narrate a genuine wow-moment discovery on camera. This maps directly
onto crate-digging: a short clip of dropping the needle on an obscure
70s/80s record, reacting, then cutting straight to the flipped/sampled
version in the DAW is exactly the authentic "process, not product" format
the platforms are currently rewarding.
- **How to use it:** film 10 seconds of the actual vinyl-find reaction for

## Remix target: Aretha Franklin — "Rock Steady" (1971, Atlantic)

- **On-brand why:** horn-driven, Bernard Purdie-style funk breakbeat,
  a bassline built for chopping, and "sock it to me" call-and-response —
  gospel grit meeting dancefloor function, the exact Cajmere/Chicago-grit-
  meets-gospel-uplift lane the essential-cuts list already trusts. Rides
  the live Aretha resurgence directly instead of an adjacent artist,
  without touching "Respect" itself (already the obvious/oversaturated
  pick — "Rock Steady" gets the same wave with more headroom).
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the intro drum break + horn stab as an
  8-bar filter-house intro (low-pass sweep reveal, matching the "1998"
  fingerprint), keep Aretha's ad-libs as fragmented vocal stabs over the
  groove rather than lead vocal, replace the original bass with a warm
  sub in C or A minor at 122–124 BPM. Hook-only edit, one drop, same
  release-safe restraint as the EWF "Fantasy" and Candi Staton flips
  already logged — personal/preview edit, not a full re-record.
- **Trending potential:** posts directly into the currently-live soul-
  revival / empowerment-edit tag cluster with real algorithmic tailwind
  from documentary/biopic press, and the reaction-template framing above
  (vinyl find → DAW flip) gives it a second, independently-trending format
  to ride at the same time.
