## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. ANOTR's "Talk to You" (disco house, feat. 54 Ultra) is the season's
default sunlit soundtrack** — #1 on the Global Dance Radio airplay chart
back in April, and now the viral default for TikTok/Reels summer content,
plus a real-world Ibiza/Pacha NYC club moment behind it. This isn't an
adjacent-genre opening like the donk or soul-revival trends of the last
two weeks — it's actual disco-house going mainstream-viral, blended with
funk, which is unusually close to home. The opportunity isn't to chase
ANOTR's specific sound (too polished/festival-adjacent for the "1998"
fingerprint) — it's that casual scrollers are primed for warm disco-funk
bass and horn stabs right now, more than any point in the last month.
- **How to use it:** whichever 75 Deep track leans hardest into a
  disco-adjacent bassline or horn stab, cut a 15-sec "groove only" clip
  (bass + horns, no build) captioned around "disco never left" or "this
  is where that sound comes from" — rides the ANOTR wave's warm-up-your-
  ears effect without imitating its polish.

**2. "Hot Dog Summer" — the anti-polish, chaos-over-perfection authenticity
trend — is outperforming curated aesthetic content this week.** Creators
are deliberately embracing awkward, unpolished, un-precious moments
instead of the manicured golden-hour aesthetic that dominated earlier in
the summer. This is a direct cultural cosign for the "deliberately
imperfect, lo-fi is the statement" ethos already in the music context file
(Moodymann/Theo Parrish lane) — the platform mood has swung toward
exactly the raw, unpolished production values that are already the
default here, not something to chase, just something to lean into
louder.
- **How to use it:** post a rough, one-take DAW session clip of a 75 Deep
  work-in-progress — visible mistakes, a loop still finding its pocket,
  no polish pass — captioned in the "process not product" register,
  rather than waiting for a clean final render. Low production effort,
  high authenticity payoff this week specifically.

## Remix target: Cheryl Lynn — "Got to Be Real" (1978, Columbia)

- **On-brand why:** this is disco-house's actual bloodline, not an
  adjacent genre — Frankie Knuckles played it at the Warehouse, and its
  David Foster/David Paich bassline and horn stabs are foundational
  Chicago-house DNA. Funky and sexy in the literal sense the brief asks
  for, squarely 1978 crate-dig territory, and it gives the ANOTR
  disco-house moment above a direct "here's where that sound actually
  comes from" narrative instead of a copycat one.
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the bassline + horn stab as a 4-bar
  filter-house intro (slow low-pass sweep open over 8 bars), lock your
  own kick/hat groove in at 122–124 BPM once it opens, keep the "real,
  real, real" vocal hook as a fragmented ad-lib stab rather than a lead
  vocal, replace the original bass with a warm analog sub in A minor or
  C minor underneath it. Hook-only edit, same release-safe restraint as
  the Zapp, Aretha, and Candi Staton flips logged the last three weeks —
  personal/preview edit, not a full re-record.
- **Trending potential:** rides the ANOTR disco-house wave directly as
  its own recognizable disco/soul standard (strong on its own merits,
  not dependent on the news cycle), while giving a built-in caption angle
  ("the original disco-house record") that's differentiated from every
  other creator just posting ANOTR's actual track.
