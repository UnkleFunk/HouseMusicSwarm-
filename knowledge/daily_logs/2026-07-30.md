## Remix target: Manu Dibango — "Soul Makossa" (1972, Fiesta/Atlantic)

- **On-brand why:** Afro-funk royalty with a horn riff and a rolling,
  syncopated bassline built for chopping — the same "rhythmic complexity
  over deep foundation" DNA that the Afro House lane in the taste profile
  already trusts, plus it's the direct ancestor of the "mama-ko mama-sa"
  hook Michael Jackson and Rihanna both later sampled, so the melodic
  fragment is recognizable on its own merits. Funky and sexy in the
  literal sense the assignment wants, squarely in the 70s crate-dig
  window, and it rhymes thematically with this week's amapiano/percussion
  wave without being an amapiano track.
- **Mash/edit approach:** loop the horn stab + "mama-ko mama-sa" vocal
  hook as a 2-bar fragment, run a low-pass filter-house intro (sweep open
  over 8 bars, matching the "1998" fingerprint), keep the original
  percussion breakdown intact underneath as the rhythmic anchor rather
  than replacing it, and drop your own warm sub in C or A minor under the
  bassline once the filter opens at 122–124 BPM. Hook-only edit, one
  drop — same release-safe restraint as the Zapp, Aretha, EWF, and Candi
  Staton flips already logged, personal/preview edit not a full re-record.
- **Trending potential:** the horn-and-chant hook is durable enough to
  land even without a news hook (decades of sample recognition), while
  the percussion-forward breakdown gives it a second, live tailwind
  riding this week's Tyla/amapiano percussion appetite.
