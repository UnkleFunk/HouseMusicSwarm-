[TREND] Trend scout for 75 Deep backlog (auto-run, 2026-08-01)

## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Parliament's "Flashlight" (1978) is having a real, non-nostalgia-post
moment on TikTok right now** — not just background audio, but active
musician engagement: bass players posting "basstok" breakdowns of Bernie
Worrell's iconic Minimoog synth-bass line, plus a "Flashlight" line-dance
format spreading independently. This is different from a generic 70s-funk
nostalgia wave (which is also still running — EWF's "September" and the
Bee Gees are both getting throwback-dance traction) because it's driven by
people geeking out on the *bassline itself*, which is exactly the kind of
audience that would recognize and reward a house flip that respects the
original part instead of burying it.
- **How to use it:** don't chop the vocal — isolate the Worrell synth-bass
  hook as a 2-bar loop and build a filter-house intro around it (low-pass
  sweep reveal, same technique as the last three picks), then let your own
  kick/hat groove lock in underneath once the filter opens. Clip idea: a
  10–15 sec "spot the sample" reveal — bass line solo first, then the
  house groove drops under it — captioned toward the basstok crowd rather
  than a general nostalgia audience.

**2. The "the saxophones are getting louder" tension-sting format is still
one of the biggest meme templates on both platforms** (originating from
the Boyz n the Hood dramatic-sax scene, now applied ironically to mundane
"something's about to go wrong" moments). It's a *format*, not a sample —
but it's a directly usable structural idea for a 75 Deep breakdown clip:
build a horn/sax stab that swells and repeats like it's about to signal
disaster, hold the tension a beat longer than expected, then cut it dead
into the drop instead of resolving it dramatically. That's a ready-made
"you know what's coming" caption hook for whichever track has a horn stab
in the breakdown.
- **How to use it:** cut a 10-12 sec clip that's just the rising horn
  stab on loop with a caption like "the saxophones are getting louder"
  timed to the tension build, then hard-cut to the drop with no
  transition — the abruptness is the joke and the flex at once.

## Remix target: Parliament — "Flashlight" (1978, Casablanca Records)

- **On-brand why:** this is about as close to the source code of
  Chicago/Detroit house as a sample gets — Bernie Worrell's synth bass on
  "Flashlight" is direct P-Funk lineage, the same DNA that runs through
  the Zapp pick from last week. It passes the "sounds like Unkle Funk
  would have played it" test on reputation alone, and unlike a generic
  70s-nostalgia pull, it's independently trending *this week* through
  musician-driven content rather than a meme format.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the Minimoog bass hook as a 2–4 bar
  loop, run the now-standard filter-house intro (8-bar low-pass sweep),
  let your own kick/hat groove take over at 122–124 BPM once it opens,
  and keep a warm sub doubling the hook underneath in the "1998"
  fingerprint key rather than replacing it outright — treat the original
  bass as the lead voice, not a texture. Hook-only, one big drop, same
  restraint as the Candi Staton / EWF / Zapp flips logged the last three
  weeks — keeps it a personal/preview edit, not a bootleg.
- **Trending potential:** rides two audiences at once — the basstok crowd
  actively dissecting the original part right now, and the broader
  disco/funk revival wave (EWF, Bee Gees) that's been building for weeks.
  Also gives you a natural bridge into the "saxophones are getting louder"
  format above if a future 75 Deep edit wants to pair a horn-stab tension
  build with this bassline underneath.
