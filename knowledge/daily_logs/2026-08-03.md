## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Disco/funk/soul revival is now a structural trend, not a one-song
flash** — throwback anchors like Earth, Wind & Fire's "September" and the
Bee Gees' "Stayin' Alive" are driving TikTok dance-edit and nostalgia
content, and multiple trend trackers are independently calling out a
nu-disco / retro-soul-fusion wave gaining ground through August. This is
the same signal that flagged EWF "Fantasy" as a still-open pick on
2026-07-20 — three weeks later it's clearly not a blip, it's a season.
- **How to use it:** any nearly-finished 75 Deep track with a filtered
  disco-string stab or a four-on-the-floor pulse under 130 BPM can be
  teased as a "disco-to-house" breakdown clip — start on the recognizable
  disco element, then let it drop into your own 909/808 groove underneath.
  The reveal *is* the content; caption around "took a disco record and
  made it house" rather than naming a specific song.

**2. House music itself is having a native "Friday night" moment on
TikTok/Reels** — creators are posting house sets and house-adjacent dance
content specifically timed to the start of the weekend, and it's
tracked as one of the platform's recurring dance-style categories right
now (alongside hip-hop and contemporary). Unlike the crossover plays in
past weeks, this doesn't require disguising anything — it's an opening to
post 75 Deep clips *as* house music, natively, into an audience already
primed to want exactly that on a Friday.
- **How to use it:** hold your strongest nearly-finished breakdown for a
  Friday-evening post, captioned plainly around "Friday house" / "start
  the weekend right" — no gimmick needed, just timing.

## Remix target: Sylvester — "You Make Me Feel (Mighty Real)" (1978, Fantasy Records)

- **On-brand why:** this isn't just a funky/sexy 70s disco cut — it's
  genealogically house music. Frankie Knuckles and the Warehouse crowd
  played disco imports like this one before "house" had a name; sampling
  it is sampling the source code of the genre this backlog is built on.
  James Wirrick's bassline is warm and rubbery in exactly the Kerri
  Chandler sense, the vocal hook is euphoric/spiritual rather than
  pop-sexy, and the original tempo (~125 BPM) sits almost exactly inside
  the "1998" fingerprint (123 BPM) — minimal stretching needed.
- **Mash/edit approach:** strip the disco strings and horns entirely, keep
  just the bassline and a chopped 2-bar fragment of "you make me feel /
  mighty real" as a call-and-response vocal, run the vocal through room
  reverb (not hall) so it sits inside the mix rather than floating above
  it. Rebuild the low end with your own 909/808 groove in C or A minor
  underneath the original bass — treat Sylvester's bass as a guest layer,
  same restraint used on the Zapp flip logged 2026-07-27. Long intro:
  8–16 bars of just bass + vocal fragment before the drums land, per the
  arrangement patience rule in the taste profile.
- **Trending potential:** rides both trends above at once — it's disco
  DNA (trend 1) turned into an unmistakably house record (trend 2) — and
  the "mighty real" hook is recognizable enough on its own that it works
  as a caption even without explaining the sample.
