## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. The nostalgia/camcorder aesthetic wave is dominating both platforms right
now** — grainy mirror selfies, scrapbook/diary formats, early-2000s filters,
plus a specific generational-comparison format ("me at the same age as my
parents," cutting your own chaotic footage against Pitbull's "Hey Baby (Drop
It to the Floor)") that's framing "look how much time has passed" as content.
This isn't a sample to chase, it's a *narrative format that's basically
already about 75 Deep* — a mailman finishing 25-year-old tracks is a
generational-gap story by definition. The technique worth stealing: old
footage/old idea vs. now, side by side, with a blunt on-screen caption doing
the emotional work.
- **How to use it:** pull actual old session material — a phone-camera clip
  of the original DAT/cassette/hard-drive sketch from 20+ years ago if it
  exists, or even just a screenshot of an ancient project file — cut against
  today's finished 75 Deep session, caption something like "started this at
  23, finishing it at 48." No polish. The rawness is the point, and it rides
  the exact aesthetic (camcorder/scrapbook, unfiltered) that's currently
  outperforming clean edits.

**2. 70s/80s funk-soul classics are getting elevated circulation again** —
Wild Cherry's "Play That Funky Music" is actively recirculating in viral
edit content, and Marvin Gaye's catalog (particularly the "Distant Lover"
side of *Let's Get It On*) is showing renewed discussion and clip activity
around its raw, pleading, live-performance energy. Distinct from the
donk/bounce wave logged two weeks ago — this one is slower, sexier, more
vocal-forward, which opens a lane for 75 Deep's more stripped-down,
after-hours tracks rather than the up-tempo ones.
- **How to use it:** whichever 75 Deep track leans slowest and most vocal/
  Rhodes-forward, cut a 15–20 sec "just the feeling" clip — no drums for the
  first 8 bars, chords and a fragment of vocal only — captioned around
  "late night" or "after hours." Ride the slow-sexy-soul attention instead
  of competing with the faster funk-edit content from trend #1.

## Remix target: Marvin Gaye — "Distant Lover" (1973, Tamla/Motown, from *Let's Get It On*)

- **On-brand why:** the exact "warm, spiritual, dark room, sweat, bodies"
  vocabulary from the taste profile — this is the song that vocabulary was
  basically written for. Deep soul, minor-leaning chord movement, a vocal
  performance built entirely on restraint-then-release (especially the 1974
  Oakland live version), and it's squarely inside the 70s crate-dig lane
  with zero EDM/festival contamination risk. Passes the "sounds like Unkle
  Funk would have played it" test as a listening record, not just a sample.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the opening Rhodes/guitar figure and the
  first vocal phrase ("I don't know exactly where...") as a 4-bar loop, keep
  it fully exposed and dry for 8 bars — no house elements yet — then bring
  in a warm sub and a patient 909-swing hat groove underneath at 118–121 BPM
  (slower than the usual 122–124 window, matching the song's own patience),
  letting the original chords keep breathing on top rather than replacing
  them. This is a "reveal," not a flip — closer to how Kerri Chandler or
  Dennis Ferrer would let a soul vocal lead for the first minute before the
  groove fully commits. Hook-only, one vocal phrase, personal-reference
  edit — same restraint as the Candi Staton, EWF, and Zapp flips logged the
  last three weeks.
- **Trending potential:** rides trend #2's slow-soul attention directly, and
  because "Distant Lover" is already a well-loved deep-cut (not
  over-exposed like the obvious Marvin Gaye singles), an edit built around
  it reads as a genuine discovery rather than chasing an oversaturated
  sample — durable appeal even after this week's specific circulation cools.
