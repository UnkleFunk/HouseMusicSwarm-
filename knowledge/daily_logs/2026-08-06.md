## Remix target: Marvin Gaye — "Sexual Healing" (1982, Columbia)

- **On-brand why:** the drum-machine groove (that dusty, syncopated Roland
  CR-8000 pattern) is a direct ancestor of the "drum machine as a soul
  instrument, not a click track" philosophy Chicago house was built on —
  it's the missing link between Motown-era soul and the 909/808 language
  already core to the fingerprint. It's literally titled and themed sexy,
  funky bass synth riff, sparse arrangement, minor-key mood — passes the
  "sounds like Unkle Funk would have played it" test cleanly.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the drum-machine intro + bass synth riff
  as an 8-bar loop, layer your own 909 hats/ride under it to modernize
  the swing without erasing the original's character, then let the "get
  it on... sexual healing" vocal phrase drop exactly once as the hook —
  no looping the full vocal. Rebuild everything else (chords, sub) fresh
  in A minor around it, 122–124 BPM, same hook-only/one-drop restraint
  used on the last three flips logged in this series.
- **Trending potential:** ties directly into both trends above — a
  "drum-machine lineage" angle (Marvin Gaye's machine → Chicago's
  909/808 tradition) is an evergreen educational-format hook that reads
  well regardless of what's charting that specific week, and it lands
  inside the Aug 27–30 festival attention window if timed as the "hold
  back" release from trend #1. Recognition risk is low — it's one of the
  most sample-familiar grooves in soul history, so even a heavily
  reworked flip reads as intentional homage, not obscurity.
