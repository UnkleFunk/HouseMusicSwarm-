seasonal cue** (fall-start audio wave, per this week's trending-audio
roundups) — the horn-stab intro and "do you remember" vocal hook are
carrying outfit-change, nostalgia, and "new season, new me" edits. This is

"Fantasy" on 7/20, Aretha "Rock Steady" on 7/26), but the trigger this time
is calendar-driven, not news-driven — a reliably annual window rather than
a one-off news cycle, which means the "how to use it" move can be reused
every September going forward.
- **How to use it:** don't touch "September" itself (oversaturated,
  already the obvious pick every fall) — instead cut a 10–15 sec clip of

  Wurlitzer stab reads close enough) landing hard on the 1, captioned
  around "seasons changing, so is the groove" — rides the *seasonal-audio
  behavior* (people are primed to want horn-stab nostalgia hooks right
  now) without competing directly against the EWF original.

**2. Anita Ward's "Ring My Bell" (1979) is mid-resurgence on Instagram**,
soundtracking outfit-check and "girl era" lifestyle Reels off its bright
disco-chime hook. Multiple house/disco-house remixes are already
circulating (Bandcamp/SoundCloud), which is itself a signal of real

there.
- **How to use it:** the synth chime riff is instantly recognizable in

  captioned "you know this one," works as a teaser cut for the remix
  target below before the full flip is finished.

## Remix target: Anita Ward — "Ring My Bell" (1979, Juana/TK Records,
written/produced by Frederick Knight)

- **On-brand why:** funky *and* literally sexy — the title, the vocal
  delivery, and the synth-bell hook are all flirtatious dancefloor
  invitation, squarely inside the 70s/80s crate-dig lane and currently
  riding real cultural heat (see trend #2). The existing remix wave is all
  commercial disco-house (polished, major-key, festival-adjacent bounce) —
  exactly the anti-pattern the taste profile rules out. That's the
  opening: nobody's done the warm, sub-heavy, minor-key deep house version
  yet, which is a genuine differentiation angle, not just a bandwagon
  jump.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the synth chime riff as a 2-bar loop and
  run it through a room-reverb, low-pass filter-house intro (8-bar sweep
  open, matching the "1998" fingerprint) — treat the chime as a guest
  hook, not the harmonic foundation. Strip the original's major-key bass
  entirely and replace it with a warm, saturated sub in A minor or C
  minor at 122–124 BPM so the chime reads as a found sample floating over
  a completely different (and much darker) harmonic bed. Keep Anita
  Ward's vocal to isolated "ring my bell" ad-lib stabs over the groove,
  never as a lead vocal — hook-only edit, personal/preview restraint,
  same as every prior week's pick.
- **Trending potential:** rides a currently-live Instagram audio wave with
  proven remix appetite (multiple disco-house versions already gaining
  traction), while the minor-key/warm-sub treatment gives it a
  differentiated angle that stands out against the commercial versions
  already saturating that wave — recognizable enough to catch casual
  scrollers, distinct enough to read as unmistakably Unkle Funk to anyone
  who knows the lineage.
