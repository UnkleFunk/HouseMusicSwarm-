## Two trends worth chasing this week

**1. Earth, Wind & Fire's "September" is spiking on Reels right on the
calendar cue** ("do you remember, the 21st night of September" — the
song reliably resurges every year as fall starts, and this week's
trending-audio roundups have it back near the top). The hook for 75 Deep
isn't the song itself — it's the *nostalgia/memory framing* that's
carrying it: "do you remember" is functionally the same emotional beat
as the mailman-finishing-25-years'-worth-of-tracks story. That's a
content format, not a sample source, and it's a short window — the
calendar relevance dies the second October hits.
- **How to use it:** don't flip the song — steal the frame. Cut a
  10–15 sec studio clip captioned like "do you remember this one?" over
  a WIP track that's been sitting unfinished the longest, audio swapped
  to your own instrumental. Post it while "September" is still riding
  the seasonal wave so the algorithm's memory-nostalgia mood matches the
  75 Deep mission statement almost word for word.

**2. Disco is having a real moment again on Reels via Anita Ward's
"Ring My Bell" (1979)** — currently trending for outfit/lifestyle and
playful-feminine content, part of a broader "disco energy" resurgence
alongside Boney M/R3HAB's "Sunny." This is a genre-wave trend like the
EWF/soul-funk one logged back in July, not a one-song fluke — which
means any 75 Deep track with a bell-tone, chime, or bright stab hook can
ride the same discovery pool.
- **How to use it:** if any nearly-finished 75 Deep track has a
  bell/chime/glockenspiel-type stab (or could take one), cut a 15–20 sec
  "groove reveal" — drums + bass in, bell stab drops on beat 1 — and
  caption it in the same disco-revival lane as the Ring My Bell edits.
  See the remix target below for a more direct flip of this one.

## Remix target: Anita Ward — "Ring My Bell" (1979)

- **On-brand why:** Frederick Knight-produced disco/boogie classic,
  Chicago-radio-era staple, sexy vocal hook ("you can ring my bell") over
  a bright, minimal, bell-tone-driven groove — it already sits in the
  disco/proto-house lineage that Trax and Prescription grew out of, and
  it passes the "sounds like Unkle Funk would have played it" test on
  the strength of the hook alone.
- **Mash/edit approach:** isolate the vocal hook + the signature bell/
  chime stab as a 2–4 bar loop. Rebuild underneath it: warm sub bass in
  C or A minor to fit the "1998" fingerprint, 909 hats with the usual
  off-beat swing, no side-chain pump under the bell hits (let the
  original's minimalism stay minimal). Keep the bassline walking rather
  than static per the Levan-style "bass as melody" approach. One drop of
  the full "you can ring my bell" phrase, not a looped hook — treat it
  as a hook edit/personal preview, same restraint as the Candi Staton and
  EWF "Fantasy" flips logged earlier this cycle.
- **Trending potential:** it's independently riding a live disco-revival
  wave on Reels this week (outfit videos, playful content), so a clip
  built around this flip drops into an existing discovery pool instead
  of needing to build recognition from zero — and the bell-tone hook
  gives it an easy visual moment (literal bell/chime graphic) for a
  15–20 sec vertical cut.
