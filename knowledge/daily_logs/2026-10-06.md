## Two trends worth chasing

**1. Diana Ross — "Upside Down" (1980) on Reels ("Upside Down" trend, Oct 2)**
Creators flip the frame, mouth the lyrics, let the transition carry it. The
record is a Chic production (Nile Rodgers / Bernard Edwards), so it's
funk-guitar + bass-driven disco: squarely Unkle Funk territory.
- **Use it:** don't sample the vocal. Borrow the *feel*: a choppy, clean
  off-beat guitar-scratch loop and a rolling octave bassline under a 75 Deep
  track. Or do a 15-30s Reel where the video flips upside down on the drop
  of a nearly-finished track. Format rides the trend, music stays original.

**2. The "Process" trend (behind-the-scenes → finished result)**

the 75 Deep story: late-night session clip (mixer, Ableton arrangement view,
mailman bag on the chair), then cut to the finished loop. One text card:
"Track 31 of 75. Done at 2am." Low cost, repeatable per track.

Also noted: "Stereo Love Funk" dance trend (TikTok), sped-up sax edits ("Saxophones
getting louder", TikTok #3). A sax stab/riff layered over a funky house groove

## Remix target: Diana Ross — "Upside Down" (1980)
- **On-brand:** Chic rhythm section = the blueprint for funky/disco house.
  Sexy, strutting, instantly recognizable.
- **Edit approach:** keep it as a DJ-tool edit, not a full remix. Extend the
  intro groove, loop the guitar/bass bar, hold the "upside down" hook for one
  big drop after a filter-sweep build, 120-124 BPM, add a warm sub and a
  Chicago-style clap/shuffle hat. Release as a free/DJ edit or Reel
  teaser only (clearance needed for any commercial release).
- **Trending potential:** audio is live on Reels right now; an edit clip
  posted within the week rides the existing sound recognition.
- **Backup candidate:** Chic — "I Want Your Love" (1978) if clearance
  or availability is a problem.
