## Two trends worth chasing

**1. "Then vs. now" progress edits (Foo Fighters "Everlong" 123K Reels, GNR "November Rain" 322K, rising)**
Before/after progress stories are the live format. This is 75 Deep's native structure.
- **How to use it:** Cut a 15s clip: raw 8-bar loop/sketch from night 1 -> the finished arrangement from the nearly-finished track, hard cut on the downbeat. Caption with the day count ("Night 31 of 75"). Use your own audio, not Everlong; the format is the trend, not the sound.

**2. "Digicam fall diaries" — soft, wistful, film-look late-night clips (The Cranberries "Linger" 117K Reels)**
Personal, low-polish daily-life clips are rewarded. Late-night studio glow, hands on the controller, a coffee gone cold.
- **How to use it:** 10–12s handheld digicam-style shots of the 75 Deep session, with the filtered, slowed intro of a nearly-finished track as the bed. Flip the wistful "Linger" mood into a funky payoff: the filter opens and the bassline drops at the end of the clip.

(Not used: Christmas sounds, which are a seasonal/brand wave, and Dame Hombro / Housewife, which are dance formats with no house fit.)

## Remix target: Alicia Myers — "You Get the Best from Me (Say, Say, Say)" (1981, MCA)
Not yet used in earlier picks (Candi Staton, EWF "Fantasy", Aretha "Rock Steady", Zapp).
- **On-brand why:** sexy, smoky early-80s boogie-soul with a rubbery bassline and a lived-in gospel-trained vocal. Fits the funky/soulful lane and the Chicago lineage without being the over-sampled obvious pick.
- **Mash/edit approach:** chop the "say, say, say" vocal hook into rhythmic stabs; speed to ~122–124 BPM (check key first and pitch-match to the track); build a new sub-heavy bassline; filter-sweep intro, one drop. Keep it a short hook-only personal/preview edit, consistent with the earlier restraint on full re-records. Clear rights before any release.
- **Trending potential:** rides the "then vs. now" progress format (vinyl/sample -> flip) and the ongoing vintage soul/funk dance-edit wave. The Ring My Bell (Anita Ward) disco revival noted in September Reels lists shows the late-70s/early-80s lane still gets traction.
