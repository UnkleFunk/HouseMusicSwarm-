## 3. Sonic Palette — The Texture Layer

### What the music should feel like

Words that map to the right sonic territory:
> **warm · dusty · hypnotic · spiritual · dark room · sweat · bodies · underground · breathing · raw · soulful · heavy · patient**

Words that signal wrong direction:
> polished · pristine · punchy · energetic · festival · anthemic · commercial · clean · modern-pop · shiny

### Core Sonic Elements

**Drums:**
- Roland TR-909 as the foundation — its hi-hat swing is non-negotiable
- TR-808 for the kick weight and sub-tonal presence
- LinnDrum snap for snares when there's a live feel
- Humanized velocity — nothing perfectly quantized
- Room on the snare, not gate-reverb
- Ride cymbal loops that breathe over the kick/hat groove

**Bass:**
- Deep, warm sub bass — sits below 80Hz, felt more than heard
- Vintage analog character: Juno-style portamento, warm saturation
- NOT: clinical 303 acid (unless in context), tight modern DI bass, anything that sounds like a plugin preset
- Movement through filter sweeps and subtle pitch automation, not arpeggio/step patterns

**Chords & Harmony:**
- Rhodes, Wurlitzer, Fender Rhodes — the house piano DNA
- Juno 106 / Prophet 5 stabs — that slightly detuned, warm shimmer
- Chord voicings: minor 7ths, minor 9ths, suspended 4ths — jazz-adjacent without being jazz
- Stabs that breathe — velocity variation, a little drift in timing
- Pad layers that wash and evolve slowly behind the chord

**Melody:**
- Sparse. A hook in house is a groove, not a melody.
- Flute or marimba one-bar phrases that come and go
- String samples that feel found, not composed
- Vocal: soulful, not pop. Fragmented phrases over the groove, not lead-vocal tracks.

**Effects & Space:**
- Room reverb, not hall reverb
- Tape saturation / warm analog warmth on the master
- Subtle delay throws on hats and percussion
- Low-pass filter automation that opens the track up gradually
- Nothing artificially wide — mono-compatible, club-system aware

### Ableton Workflow Orientation

- **Loop-based development:** Start with the groove loop, let it breathe before adding anything
- **Arrangement patience:** Long intros are features, not problems. 2-bar intro in a demo = wrong vibe.
- **Layering philosophy:** Less is more until it's obviously lacking. Add one element at a time.
- **Mix approach:** Low-end is primary. If the kick and bass don't work together first, nothing else matters.
- **Reference early:** Bounce and reference against a trusted track mid-session

### Instruments & Devices (Ableton-native preferences)

- **Analog** — for warm bass lines and lead synth work
- **Operator** — FM layering under Rhodes-style chords, subtle bell tones
- **Sampler / Simpler** — for chopped drum breaks, soul samples, found sounds
- **Drum Rack** — built from one-shots, not stock kits. 909 samples, 808 samples, processed breaks.

### What "Blowing His Mind" Looks Like

A creative AI output that hits right will have:

## 6. Anti-Patterns — The Explicit NO List

These are disqualifying in any context — creative proposals, recommendations, generated sounds, or AI output:

- Side-chain pumping as a featured effect (subtle ducking for mix clarity is fine)
- Build → drop structure borrowed from EDM
- Vocal chops as the melodic hook of a track
- Clap-on-every-beat energy
- Any sound that evokes Ultra Records, festival main stage, or radio Top 40
- Over-polished, transparent production (analog imperfection is correct)
- Arpeggiators running obvious major-key patterns
- Mid-range-heavy mixdowns (this music lives in the lows)
- The phrase "big room" describing anything good
